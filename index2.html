What in the World is a Trigger?

At its core, a trigger is an automatic action. This section explains the concept using a simple real-world analogy to build your foundational understanding before we dive into the technical details.

Imagine a motion-sensor light. Two simple things happen:

1

The Event

You walk into a dark room.

2

The Action

The light automatically turns on.

A MySQL Trigger is just like that!

It's a special, stored procedure that automatically runs when a specific database event happens on a table.

The Superpowers (Why Use Them?)

Triggers aren't just a technical curiosity; they solve real-world problems. This section highlights the three main "superpowers" that triggers bring to your database, turning it from a simple data store into a smart, automated system.

🛡️

Enforce Data Integrity

Act as ultimate guardians, preventing bad data from ever entering your tables. For example, "No negative prices allowed!"

⚙️

Automate Follow-up Actions

Your trusty sidekicks, handling repetitive tasks. For example, "New user? Add them to the mailing list table automatically!"

📸

Create Audit Trails

Like security cameras, logging every important change. For example, "Who changed the product price to $999?!"

Superhero Missions

Mission 1: The Price Guardian

Your task is to prevent anyone from setting a negative price for a product. We'll use a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger to inspect and fix the data before it's saved.

CREATE TRIGGER prevent_negative_price BEFORE UPDATE ON products FOR EACH ROW BEGIN -- If the NEW price is less than zero... IF NEW.price < 0 THEN -- ...set the NEW price to zero instead! SET NEW.price = 0; END IF; END;

Result: Your product prices are always safe and sound!

Mission 2: The Super-Sleuth

Your mission is to log every time an employee's salary is changed. We'll use an `AFTER UPDATE` trigger to create an audit trail after a change has been successfully made.

CREATE TRIGGER log_salary_change AFTER UPDATE ON employees FOR EACH ROW BEGIN -- If the old salary is different from the new one... IF OLD.salary <> NEW.salary THEN -- ...insert a record into our spy log! INSERT INTO salary_audits(employee_id, old_salary, new_salary, change_time) VALUES(OLD.id, OLD.salary, NEW.salary, NOW()); END IF; END;

Result: You have a perfect history of every single salary change!

The Kryptonite! (Things to Watch Out For)

Even superheroes have weaknesses. While powerful, triggers should be used with caution. This section covers the common pitfalls to help you use them wisely and avoid unexpected problems in your application.

🤫

Hidden Logic

Triggers work silently in the database, which can confuse developers who don't know they exist. Always document your triggers!

🐢

Performance

A complex trigger on a busy table can slow things down. Keep them simple and efficient!

tangled_yarn

The Domino Effect

Triggers can call other triggers, which can become complicated and hard to debug. Be careful of cascading triggers!
